Dec. 5th, 1919

Mr. Hammond,
Dear Sir,

[Stamp: CUSHMAN INDIAN SCHOOL DEC 8 1919 TACOMA, WASHINGTON]

I am miting regarding my children.
I have been sick and not able to be out, but am better now.
And feel worried over my children.
There is lots of sickness here now.
And you are responsible for the children I ask that you notify me when my children are sick, if they are bad off, I can go down and stay with them until they are better.

INDORSEMENTS.

Applicable laws relating to the transfer of Indian children from reservations and schools are as follows:
That hereafter no Indian child shall be sent from any Indian reservation to a school beyond the State or Territory in which said reservation is situated without the voluntary consent of the father or mother of such child if either of them are living... (28 Stats., p. 906.)
Provided, That hereafter no Indian child shall be taken from any school in any State or Territory to a school in any other State against its will or without the written consent of its parents. (29-Stats., p. 348.)
That no Indian pupil under the age of fourteen years shall be transported at Government expense to any Indian school beyond the limits of the State or Territory in which the parents of such child reside or of the adjoining State or Territory. (35 Stat. L., 781.)
The rules provide that-
A pupil who has been regularly enrolled in a nonreservation school must not be taken to any other nonreservation school without the consent of both superintendents and the Commissioner of Indian Affairs...
An Indian boy or girl 18 years old and over may, without the consent of parents or others, personally sign the application form...
Compliance must be given to all orders and instructions promulgated in connection with the new course of study issued December 1, 1915.
The information as to public school facilities called for in the certificate of the Superintendent... must be given...

DEPARTMENT OF THE INTERIOR
UNITED STATES INDIAN SERVICE

Umatilla Indian School,
Pendleton, Oregon. Oct. 13, 1919.

[Stamp: CUSHMAN INDIAN SCHOOL RECEIVED OCT 1 6 1919 TACOMA, WASHINGTON]

Sup't. E. H. Hammond,
Cushman Indian School,
Tacoma, Washington.

Dear Mr. Hammond:
Your letter of October 11th regarding the Cornoyer children is at hand.
I am personally acquainted with these children and with their mother. The mother does not belong to this reservation, and I do not know whether she has Indian blood or whether she has not. The father, now deceased, was a Walla Walla Indian of about 1/4 degree Indian, who was allotted. These children are enrolled on our records and all have received tentative allotments, under regular Act of Congress authorizing allotments here. Their father was a full citizen before his death as he had received patent in fee for his allotment.
If these children take the status of their father, as citizens, they are not entitled to federal aid in school work. However I am inclined to believe the Department would take the view, since they are now tentative allottees on the Umatilla reservation, that their allotments are held in trust, and that they will be considered as entitled to federal aid for school purposes so long as the government is holding their land in trust.
This question was placed before the Commissioner of Indian Affairs a month or two ago and I asked for a decision in view of the recent amendments to Indian school rules. No reply has been received however.
It is my opinion that Mrs. Gilman is not able to pay tuition for her children.

Yours very truly,
[Signature]
Superintendent.
ELS/AMH
