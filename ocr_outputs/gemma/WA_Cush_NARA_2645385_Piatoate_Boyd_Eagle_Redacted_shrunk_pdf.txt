Cushman Indian School
Tacoma, Wash., March 24, 1920

Camp Commander
Camp Lewis,
Washington

Sir:

Some of our Indian boys are desirous of enlisting in the army; two have already enlisted within the last week. Inasmuch as these boys have been entrusted to my care, I feel that it is my duty to ask you to delay the enlistment of these boys until they can secure permission from their parents.

I desire to call your attention to the enlistment of Boyd Eagle who joined the 35th Infantry, Company F, last week. Our records show this boy to be sixteen years of age. This age record is correct as I have a copy of the Colville census which shows the date of his birth to be 1904. I have also his application for enrollment which is signed by his father, dated September 13, 1917, which shows his age to have been twelve years at that time.

As there is a likelihood that some of our boys may not give you their correct age, I would appreciate your co-operation in securing some evidence as to their correct ages before their enlistment has been made.

Very respectfully,

Special Agent in Charge.

Dear Mr. Peel:

I have your letter advising me that Boyd Eagle has without your approval enlisted in the United States Army at Camp Lewis. I regret that Boyd failed to get your approval as judging from what I have observed of him the Army is the place for him.

It is true that his father is dead and his mother I believe lives on the Yakima Reservation, when she is at home. As a matter of fact she wanders around considerable from one place to another. The records of this office are somewhat confusing as to Boyd's age. The census show him to be born in 1904, however the allotment rolls, which is regarded as more authentic, show him to be born in 1903. Judging from his size, I am inclined to believe this is the proper year of birth.

If Boyd has received the consent of his mother I know of no objection to his entering the army. However, I do not approve of his method of leaving your school. If his enlistment in the army should stand, I have no objection to you turning over any funds that may be deposited to his credit. I am returning your file in this case.

Very truly yours,
[Signature: Fred C Morgan]
Superintendent.

INDORSEMENTS.
The laws relating to the transfer of Indian children from reservations and schools are as follows:
That hereafter no Indian child shall be sent from any Indian reservation to a school beyond the State or Territory in which said reservation is situated without the voluntary consent of the father or mother of such child if either of them are living, and if neither of them are living without the voluntary consent of the next of kin of such child... (28 Stats., p. 906.)
Provided, That hereafter no Indian child shall be taken from any school in any State or Territory to a school in any other State against its will or without the written consent of its parents. (29 Stats., p. 348.)
That no Indian pupil under the age of fourteen years shall be transported at Government expense to any Indian school beyond the limits of the State or Territory in which the parents of such child reside or of the adjoining State or Territory. (35 Stat. L., 781.)
The rules provide that-
A pupil who has been regularly enrolled in a nonreservation school must not be taken to any other nonreservation school without the consent of both superintendents and the Commissioner of Indian Affairs...
An Indian boy or girl 18 years old and over may, without the consent of parents or others, personally sign the application form...

This form is to be used only in transfers from reservations or Indian schools to nonreservation schools.

Dear friend;

I have your letter of January 24, 1920, and I am glad to hear from you. I am sorry that you do not feel well, but I am sure that if you work hard at your studies and play hard during the periods of recreation, you will be happy, and your health will be as good as possible.

You inquire regarding Moses Broncheau. He is seen occasionally around Nespelem, but is not at the Agency any longer. He served a sentence imposed upon him by the Indian Court, and I believe is now behaving himself.

I note that you request $45.00 for clothing, and upon reference to your ledger account, P-62, I find that you have a balance to your credit of $247.53. On September 12, when you started to school, this fall, $40.00 was drawn on this account, and on October 30, $25.00 additional was drawn. If Mr. Peel recommends that you be allowed $45.00 additional, I shall be glad to consider this matter further, but we must all remember that a bank account that is not growing, will surely be exhausted if subjected to a steady drain, and if you can get along without an expensive overcoat for the remainder of the winter, I believe that you will get more satisfaction by using your money in some other way at a later date.

I want you to be happy and well, and to get all possible good you can from the balance of the school year at Cushman. It is pleasant that you like your disciplinarian, and it is a very good plan for to hope to attend Haskell Institute next year. Mr. Peel will be very glad to consult with you about this, I am sure, and if you will tell him just what you are thinking about, I am sure he will give you helpful ideas. I hear occasionally from the other Colville boys at Cushman, and recently Patsy Moses wrote me, and from all reports the boys are doing very well.

The basket ball at the Agency hall is being carried on by the returned students who are still here, Eddie Moses, James Yamout and others who live near the Agency.

I am always glad to hear from you, and remember that I shall take further action on your request for money, upon receipt of word from Mr. Peel, as to his recommendation.

With best wishes, I am
Very truly your friend,
[Signature: Fred C Morgan]
Superintendent.

INDORSEMENTS.
The laws relating to the transfer of Indian children from reservations and schools are as follows:
That hereafter no Indian child shall be sent from any Indian reservation to a school beyond the State or Territory in which said reservation is situated without the voluntary consent of the father or mother of such child if either of them are living, and if neither of them are living without the voluntary consent of the next of kin of such child... (28 Stats., p. 906.)
Provided, That hereafter no Indian child shall be taken from any school in any State or Territory to a school in any other State against its will or without the written consent of its parents. (29 Stats., p. 348.)
That no Indian pupil under the age of fourteen years shall be transported at Government expense to any Indian school beyond the limits of the State or Territory in which the parents of such child reside or of the adjoining State or Territory. (35 Stat. L., 781.)
The rules provide that-
A pupil who has been regularly enrolled in a nonreservation school must not be taken to any other nonreservation school without the consent of both superintendents and the Commissioner of Indian Affairs...
An Indian boy or girl 18 years old and over may, without the consent of parents or others, personally sign the application form...

Compliance must be given to all orders and instructions which have been promulgated in connection with the new course of study issued December 1, 1915.
The information as to public school facilities called for in the certificate of the Superintendent (third page hereof) must be given...
