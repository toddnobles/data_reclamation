Cushman Indian School
Tacoma, Wash., Oct. 10, 1919

The Commissioner of Indian Affairs
Washington, D. C.

My dear Mr. Commissioner:

I am returning herewith the letter from Mrs. Josephine Goodman, 811 So. 11th St., Tacoma, Washington, requesting the continued enrollment of her three children at the Cushman Indian School.

Mrs. Goodman is Quinaielt Indian, degree of blood uncertain, altho she claims [blacked out] blood. She has received her patent in fee since the enrollment of her children at Cushman and the children have never been allotted. Mrs. Goodman supports herself as Cook in a boarding house located on Eleventh Street, Tacoma, and is in no position to under take the support of her three children as her wages are barely sufficient for her own support and would not permit her maintaining a separate home for the children.

The children are enrolled as [blacked out] Indian blood, but would pass anywhere as white children. I am attaching herewith a copy of my letter to Mrs. Goodman regarding the release of her children from Cushman. The children are still at Cushman, however, as I could not drop them out into the street and the mother is unable to receive them.

Very respectfully,

Superintendent.

EHH:MG
Enc.

INDORSEMENTS.

Applicable laws relating to the transfer of Indian children from reservations and schools are as follows:
That hereafter no Indian child shall be sent from any Indian reservation to a school beyond the State or Territory in which said reservation is situated without the voluntary consent of the father or mother of such child if either of them are living... (28 Stats., p. 906.)
Provided, That hereafter no Indian child shall be taken from any school in any State or Territory to a school in any other State against its will or without the written consent of its parents. (29 Stats., p. 348.)
That no Indian pupil under the age of fourteen years shall be transported at Government expense to any Indian school beyond the limits of the State or Territory in which the parents of such child reside or of the adjoining State or Territory. (35 Stat. L., 781.)

The rules provide that—
A pupil who has been regularly enrolled in a nonreservation school must not be taken to any other nonreservation school without the consent of both superintendents and the Commissioner of Indian Affairs...
An Indian boy or girl 18 years old and over may, without the consent of parents or others, personally sign the application form...

Compliance must be given to all orders and instructions promulgated in connection with the new course of study issued December 1, 1915.

The information as to public school facilities called for in the certificate of the Superintendent (third page hereof) must be given...
