Quileute Day School,
MORA, WASH.
June 20, 1919,

[Stamp: CUSHMAN INDIAN SCHOOL RECEIVED JUN 24 1919 TACOMA, WASHINGTON]

Mr EH Hammond, Supt
Cushman Indian School,
Tacoma, Wash.

Dear Sir:
It is found advisable to place this money to the credit of Amy Jackson, and not more than fifty cents should be drawn out by her at one time. If she deserts from the school she could not reach Seattle with fifty cents and continue her journey.

I have already had some inquiries as to whether she was coming home for vacation or not. The parties are the ones wanting her hand in marriage. I would rather have her away another year, as she is a pest and will be constantly in mischief.

Please consider this confidential,
Yours truly H.T. Markishtum

[Handwritten note on right side]:
N.B.
Incase of sickness, if quite serionus, ask parents to come after her and have them promise to return her before school opens and to discipline her at home.
Have had reports of her being in the hospital,
H.T.M

**Page 9**

5-1142
DEPARTMENT OF THE INTERIOR
UNITED STATES INDIAN SERVICE
Mora, Wash., Jan. 18, 1919.

Mr. E. H. Hammond, Supt.
Cushman Trade School,
Tacoma, Wash.

Dear Sir:
Herewith are enclosed letters for Amy Jackson. They were placed in my care at the postoffice by the postmistress at Mora.

I would advise that these letters be opened by yourself or the matron in charge at her quarters, as the character of the letters can then be determined, whether the letters are for pure friendship, that they may be for a higher ideal or worthy of delivery to her.

Amy is inclined to be weak morally and by strict discipline her morals may be changed, with surroundings foreign from an Indian village, such as found in a boarding or training school. Her guardians (step-father and a deaf mother) lack control and discipline over her. Her home surroundings are not elevating. She escapes at her will from home into questionable company.

It is my advise that she be kept in school during vacation. She roamed here and there last summer without any escort or chaperone, giving Doctor Woods, (superintendent), myself and our police much trouble and annoyance.

At different times during her stay here and at Neah Bay for her vacation from Cushman, young men approached us for consent to marry her and being too young they were advised not to annoy as she was not of age yet.

I hope that you will not take this as an intrusion upon Amy's character, on your time or affairs.

Yours truly,
H.T. Markishtum,
Teacher

**Page 15**

Clear Water
Wash.
12-15-18.

Dear Amy.
I'm going fine in here. I'm enjoying myself every way. I haven't got a letter from you ever since I got over here, what is the matter are you dead, or alive yet, tell me. how is the people over there, I heard some of them died, tell me who died so I will know, people over at hoquiam are just dying by sickness doctor don't know what kind of sickness it is, just took sick over night and died. the hospital is just full, and too great big hall. And we are going to have a big times over here. Bill Sam he is going to kill one moose haha. I wish you was here. we are going to have it to-morrow.

And we are going to taholah Christmas to have a good time. What are you going to do Christmas. I will send you and postal card and that's all I'm going to say
So good by I'm going to Cut wood. Ha Ha.

Yours truly,
Crist Morganoth
Clear Water, PO.
