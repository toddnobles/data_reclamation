Dear friend;- Yours rec'd and was sure a happy boy, I was so happy that I forget that I was hungry, and I lost all my sharp appetite at the time. I got it before dinner, I knew that I'd hear from you so I went right up to the office as soon as I come home from the woods. I forget to wash up seven, and I was about the last one in the dinning room, but why talk about it, it can't always, only once x week, till we meet at bidou, oh! boy I can't hardly wait I can't count how many more days there is till 1920, but it seems that it is a whole year or a hundred miles away, but it will come all right all right You know what I mean, sure you'll be happy I'll be still happier. My seven year hard luck is to end in a few days

5-1142
DEPARTMENT OF THE INTERIOR
UNITED STATES INDIAN SERVICE

U.S. Indian School Neah Bay Wn.
April 1st, 1920.

Mr. C. V. Peel,
Cushman Indian School.
Tacoma Washington.

Dear Mr. Peel;-
This is to introduce Mr. Washington Irving, one one of our most progressive Indians of Neah Bay.
Mr. Irving has a daughter attending Cushman, and she has written him to the effect that she has been sick for some time, and that she would like to come home.
I have given Mr Irving permission to visit Cushman, and If you see fit to let Martha, come home with her father it will meet with my approval.

Very respectfully,
[Signature]
Supt, & .S.D.A.

Dear Miss Martha:
I wouldn't write letters to boys telling them that you are lonesome for them. If you wish to write to a boy friend, there is no harm in it provided you do not throw yourself at the boy or beg him for money. That is not ladylike and I want my girls to be ladies.
