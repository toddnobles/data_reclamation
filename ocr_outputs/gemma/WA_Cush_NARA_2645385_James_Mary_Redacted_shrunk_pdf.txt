DEPARTMENT OF THE INTERIOR
UNITED STATES INDIAN SERVICE
Colville Agency, Nespelem, Wash., July 21, 1919.

Superintendent E. H. Hammond,
Cushman Indian School,
Tacoma, Wash.

Dear Mr. Hammond:
You failed to enclose copy of the correspondence you had with reference to the trio, who were guilty of check raising. Your letter, however, contains sufficient information. Since the girls agree to remain in school until excused by the banker, I am of the opinion that they should remain with you during the vacation, otherwise they will be getting off too easy. I respectfully suggest that you write the parents of Mary James and Collette Martin, advising them that the girls are contented, well and getting along very nicely. This will have a tendency to put them at ease. Then perhaps they will cease bombarding you with requests for the girls to return for the vacation. I will leave the matter wholly to your judgement.

Yours very truly,

Superintendent

**Page 14: LETTER**
Cushman Indian School
Tacoma, Wash., July 28, 1919

Dear Mrs. James:
I wrote to Mr. Morgan telling why Mary did not return home for the vacation. I did not punish her for running away. The banker told the girls that he would not prosecute them if they promised to remain in school until he excused them or until they had finished their course.
Mary appears to be contented. She is certainly very well and is getting along nicely. She was promoted to Grade VI and tells me she is very glad she returned to school although during the vacation she is sometimes lonesome when the other girls are out picking berries. Mary went one day, but does not like it as it is too hot for her.
I will take good care of Mary's health and will do all I can to keep her well and happy.

Very sincerely yours,
Superintendent.

FOR FILE

Mr. E. H. Hammond, Supt.
Cushman Indian School,
Tacoma, Washington.

Dear Mr. Hammond:
Reference is made to your telegram of January 15, 1919, (Mary James and Colett Martin raised and cashed check unless they return and settle this bank at once, charge of forgery and altering check will be made by bank), I at once sent for the girls.
Mary James has just left the office, she stated that Viola Reynolds conceived the idea of raising the check refered to in your telegram and advised her, Mary, to do so. Mary states that she altered the check by adding a cipher to the figure (5), and adding the letter (y) to the word five, making it read Fifty dollars and no/100, ($50.00). Mary further states that Viola forged Mary's name in endorsing the check, that she, Viola cashed the check and gave Mary $5.00 keeping $45.00 to cover the expenses of herself and Mary Iswald to Wenatchee.
The only part that I can learn that Colett Martin took in the matter was that she advised Mary to raise the check, stating that it could be done and they would not be caught. I have not been able to see Colett, but as soon as I have had an interview with her I will write you again.
Mary James has agreed to return to Cushman and explain the entire transaction, of course I advised her to do this, telling her it was my opinion that if she were truthful in the matter that you would assist her in having the matter properly adjusted with bank officials.

Very respectfully,

Superintendent.

**Page 24: STATEMENT**
File
CUSHMAN INDIAN SCHOOL, TACOMA, WASH.
January 15, 1919.

I, Viola Runnels, do hereby voluntarily make the following statements which are true to the best of my knowledge.
We, (Nancy & Mary James, Mary Iswald, Colette Martin and myself) were up in the dormitory. We were going to run away Sunday night but didn't get away until Monday morning, October 2d. Mary James said that she never had enough money and didn't know what she could do with a check for only $5. Then she said; "Let us change it into $50." I said: "Would we get caught?" and she said: "No, because I will change my name different and we won't get caught." She then wanted to know what we would do and I told her we would do anything that you will and so did Mary Iswald. Then she said, "How will we rub the $5. and make it $50. I can put the naught right by the five and make it fifty." I told her it was all right and she asked Mary Iswald, and she told her the same. Then she tok a knife and scraped the "e" in five and changed it to fifty. Then she changed the name on the check to Margret Jameson and signed it on the back that way. I went downstairs then but the check was fixed before I went away.
The morning we ran away, we went out the end of the grounds and Mary James led the way around the streets until we came to the Puyallup road then we took a bus and got to Puyallup. We didn't know where we wanted to go but finally we took another bus to Sumner. When we got to Sumner we asked the way to the depot and Mary James asked me to take the check in and get it cashed. When I asked the agent to cash the check he asked me how much it was for and I told him fifty dollars. He said he couldn't cash such a large check. Mary James had some silver and greenbacks besides the check and when I couldn't get it cashed she said we would go to get something to eat and then buy some hats. She and I wanted to buy a hat. When we go through eating, we went to buy the hats and after we had them picked out, she didn't have enough money to pay for my hat so I took the check to buy the hat with. The lady asked us whose check it was and I said it was Mary James. Then Mary James paid the lady for my hat and took the money herself. She gave me $10 and she gave Mary Iswald $10, but when we got outside again, she took my $10 and said I might lose it. Mary Iswald did not have anything to do with the raising of the check but she was present when the check was cashed and shared in the sum of $10. When Mary James divided the money, she said "Now if we get caught, you will each have to pay $10.

[Signature] Viola Runnels.

**Page 25: LETTER**
Cushman Indian School,
Tacoma, Wash, Jan. 16, 1919.

The First National bank,
Hoquiam, Washington.

My Dear Mr. Reynolds:
I am returning herewith the check for five ($5.00) dollars raised to fifty ($50.00). There is no doubt that Mary James raised the check, or aided and abetted in raising the check and received and distributed the money.
She ran away from school telling one of her friends that if "they" found out about the check, "they" would give her the "dickens". She is at Keller, Washington. I have written her superintendent and there will be no trouble in having the money refunded immediately.
If Mary alone were implicated, I would let her take her medicine, but there is a minor, who did not realize the seriousness of the offense until it was pointed out to her.
If the refund of the money is satisfactory, it will be made through her Superintendent.

Very truly yours,
Superintendent

**Page 26: LETTER**
THE FIRST NATIONAL BANK OF HOQUIAM, WASH.
January 13, 1919

Ernest Hammond, Superintendent
Cushman Indian School
Tacoma, Washington

Dear Sir:
We have your letter of January 10th regarding a check numbered 932 issued to Mary James in the sum of $5.00. We hand you herewith this cancelled check for your inspection.
After receiving your letter, we inspected the check and immediately noticed that the name of the payee had been changed and that the amount had been raised to $50.00. This was not noticed at the time the check was cashed.
We are sending the check to you thinking that you might have some idea as to who changed it.
The endorsement on the back is undoubtedly a forgery. Would it be possible for you to find out whether or not this writing is that of Mary James?
We will credit your account $45.00 to cover this check and will change the amount of it on our statement to read $5.00 instead of $50.00.
After you have inspected the check kindly return it to us, at your earliest convenience, and we will endeavor to collect our money from the bank who originally cashed it.

Yours very truly,
The First National Bank of Hoquiam
