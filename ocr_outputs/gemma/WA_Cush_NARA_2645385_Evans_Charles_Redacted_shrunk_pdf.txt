**Chas Evans**
Koyukuk Station Alaska
Oct. 6, 1919

Supt. Cushman School
Tacoma Washington.

Dear Sir; -Your letter regarding the tuition has been received, I do not know your informers regarding my financial standing but people seem to think if a man has a store he is coining money.

I was a heavy looser in the flood two years ago and do not feel able to pay that amount and may have to bring the boys home in the spring.

Respectfully,
**J. W. Evans**

**Page 4**

**Chas Evans**
Cushman Indian School
Tacoma, Wash., March 29, 1920

Harwood Hall, Supt.
Salem Indian School
Chemawa, Oregon

My dear Mr. Hall:

There are enclosed application blanks, partially filled in by J. W. Evans, for the enrollment of his three boys in your school.

These boys are now enrolled at Cushman and desire transfer to Chemawa. Their original enrollment expires in June, this year. These blanks were sent to Mr. Evans some time ago for his execution and signature, but when he returned them, it appears that he over looked signing them. Believing that this matter properly will come within your jurisdiction, I am sending them on to you for the necessary action.

Very respectfully,
Special Agent in Charge.

OHK:MG
CC to J. W. Evans
Koyukuk, Alas.

It will cost about $25.00 to send your three boys to Chemawa.

**[Letter 1]**
DEPARTMENT OF THE INTERIOR
UNITED STATES INDIAN SERVICE
SALEM INDIAN TRAINING SCHOOL
CHEMAWA, OREGON
November 18th 1919

Mr. E. H. Hammond,
Sup't Cushman Indian School,
Tacoma, Washington.

Dear Mr. Hammond:

I am in receipt of a letter from Mr. J. W. Evans of Koyukuk Station, Alaska, asking that I admit his three boys who are enrolled at Cushman for the reason that you cannot keep them longer. While we are filled full yet the father seems so anxious for them to attend school and if you do not intend to keep them we will try to make room for them, providing they are of sufficient size.

Please advise me.
With best wishes.

Sincerely yours,
**Harwood Hall**
Superintendent.

[Stamp: CUSHMAN INDIAN SCHOOL RECEIVED NOV 2 1 1919 TACOMA, WASHINGTON]

**[Letter 2]**
Cushman Indian School
Tacoma, Washington, Nov. 21, 1919

Harwood Hall, Supt.
Salem Indian School
Chemawa, Oregon

My dear Mr. Hall:

Replying to yours of November 18th:

Mr. J. W. Evans of Koyukuk Station, Alaska, wrote and asked that the boys be allowed to continue in school until spring. This was in reply to my letter requesting tuition from him.

I have authority from the Commissioner to retain the Alaskan pupils that I have already enrolled until the end of the school year. I will talk Chemawa to them and will do my best to influence them to enter it at the close of their term here next spring.

They are now 8th grade boys, 13 to 17 years of age and are very desirable pupils.

Very truly yours,
Superintendent.

EHH:MG

**Page 18**

Cushman Indian School
Tacoma, Wash., Nov. 21, 1919

Mr. J. W. Evans,
Koyukuk, Alaska

My dear Mr. Evans:

I beg to acknowledge receipt of your letter regarding your three boys.

The Commissioner has approved my retaining them until the end of the present school year, when they will graduate from this school. That doesnot mean, however, that their education will be complete but I strongly urge you to have them enter Chemawa in order to complete the full Vocational course which will carry them through the 9th and 10th grades.

Wilfred and Charles have completed two years in Engineering and are now enrolled in the third year. It will be very much to their advantage to review the third year next year and to take the 4th year as they are especially interested in that kind of work.

Richard is at present in grade six and has had a taste of carpentry, blacksmithing, painting, and gardening. He is not particularly interested in anything yet except his music and having a good time.

Very sincerely yours,
Superintendent.

EHH: MG
