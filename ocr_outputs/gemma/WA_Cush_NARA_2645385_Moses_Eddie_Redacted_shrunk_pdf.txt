SPECIAL NOTE.
This form must be executed in duplicate when a child is transferred from a reservation to a nonreservation school. The Superintendent of the nonreservation school will retain the original for his files, and the duplicate shall be deposited in the reservation school records. The reservation superintendent should send to the Commissioner of Indian Affairs his certificate as provided by law. All the blanks must be properly filled in every case.
If the information called for on any part of the blank is not known, that fact should be stated. No space should be left unfilled. Whether the parents are living or dead, their names must be given.
The person who signs the blank as consenting to the transfer should indicate his relation to the applicant by marking out the word "parent," "guardian," or "next of kin," leaving unmarked only the title appropriate to the signer.

Dear Mr. Hammond:

Judge Awhi, stepfather of Eddie Moses is at this office and requests me to write you and ask that Eddie Moses' enrollment at your school, be cancelled, and that you permit me to send for Eddie, who is hiding on the Yakima, to return to his home.

Eddie's mother and stepfather are very much worried for fear Eddie will get into trouble of some sort, and his desertion from the school has caused them considerable anxiety and worry. Judge Awhi and his mother have both agreed and desire to have Eddie Moses punished because of his desertion and other offenses committed.

Judging from the trouble Eddie has caused you, it is my opinion that your school will be better off without him, since he appears to be a rather leading character and is no doubt in a measure responsible for the desertion of Lawrence Picard, and Willie Dave. I do not care to send for Eddie to return to this Agency without it meets with your approval and respectfully request that you advise me as to your attitude in this matter at the earliest possible date.

Very truly yours,
[Signature] Fred C Morgan
Superintendent.

Colville Agency, Nespelem, Wash.
December 18, 1918.

Mr. E. H. Hammond,
Superintendent, Cushman School,
Tacoma, Wash.

My dear Mr. Hammond:

I have just had a talk with Eddie Moses, due to the fact that his step-father, Owhi, who is one of the Judges of the Court of Indian Offences at this Agency, who sent him to the office to explain to me the part he played in an unsavory deal he got into at the time he wrote you a very objectionable letter.

Eddie was very frank in his statements. He advised me that Peter Moses and others were involved. Peter told Eddie of his (Peter's) talk with you, so for that reason he did not mention Peter's part in the deal in his letter which he wrote you, apologizing and requesting that you forgive him. I impressed on his mind the fact that the matter is still hanging over his head and might be taken up at any time he failed to walk in the proper path. It is my purpose, after he can be spared from assisting in feeding cattle for a sick relative, to have the Indian Court give him thirty days in jail. The remembrance of thirty days on the wood pile may prevent him from getting into similar trouble in the future.

There are two or three more pupils, good ones, that I desire to return to school in the near future. Please advise me if the Influenza Epidemic has left your institution. If so I shall try and get them off shortly after the first of the year.

Very respectfully,

Fred C Morgan
Superintendent.

5-240
VACATION REQUEST FOR PUPIL.

We, the undersigned, respectfully request that leave of absence be granted Eddie Moses from June 15, 1918 to Sept 5, 1918 to enable him to visit his home. We hereby agree to pay all expenses, including round-trip railroad fares, sleeping-car fares, meals, lodging, stage fares, telegraph and telephone messages, and other incidental expenses connected with the visit. We further agree that said pupil shall be returned to the Cushman school on or before the last day of the leave of absence.

We further agree to deposit with the superintendent of the school sufficient money to defray all expenses to be incurred, and should the pupils, or any one of them, fail to return to school at such time as may be agreed upon by the parents or guardian and the superintendent of the school in which the pupils are enrolled, the amount of money remaining with the superintendent, necessary to the return transportation of the pupil or pupils so neglecting to return, shall be forfeited to the Government; provided, however, that in all cases where it can be shown that there were justifiable reasons for the failure of the return of the pupil the forfeiture of the funds shall not take place.

If the pupil neglecting to return is from a reservation, the superintendent of the reservation shall certify to the reasons for the nonreturn of the pupil or pupils. If a pupil's home is not on a reservation, then the certificate of some reputable, disinterested citizen of the community may be requested as to the reasons for the nonreturn of the pupil or pupils.

[Signatures: Helpercss, Charley Bot.]
[Signature: Awhi Step]

Date: June 1, 1918.

NOTE TO DISBURSING OFFICERS.—All receipts under this agreement must be accounted for officially as Individual Indian Money. However, any forfeitures must be transferred to Miscellaneous Receipts, Class I, and deposited in the Treasury to the credit of the United States.

Mr. E. H. Hammond,
Supt. Cushman Indian School,

Dear Mr. Hammond:

Awhi, one of the judges of the Indian court at this agency, called at the office today and asked me to write you and request that his stepson, Eddie Moses, be permitted to return home for the purpose of assisting his parents in seeding about 200 acres of land. Awhi, like the other Indians I have recently written about, is one of the progressive Indians and is farming a considerable area. He agrees to send Eddie back after he has finished seeding, which he thinks will take about one month.

I respectfully recommend that the application be given favorable consideration. In the event you approve of letting the various boys return to Colville for the purpose of assisting their parents in putting in the spring crop and funds are necessary please advise me and money will be sent you from their bank accounts to defer their expenses home.

In this connection I desire to state that the Indians in the vicinity of the agency are, I understand, unusually active and I believe will put in the largest acreage that has ever been sowed by Indians in this vicinity. This is very gratifying and were it not for the fact that they are making an unusual effort I would hesitate to recommend that their children be permitted to return for the purpose of assisting them.

Very respectfully,

Fred C Morgan
Superintendent.

5-297 i
FARM CARPENTRY.
RECORD CARD—PREVOCATIONAL DIVISION.

Each boy should devote 15 weeks to the work of this department during the three prevocational years. When he has accomplished the work as outlined in the course of study in a satisfactory manner, a suitable rating should be recorded. Ratings must be based on a practical application as well as a knowledge of the principles involved.
Ratings should be excellent (E), good (G), fair (F). If pupil's work is not worthy of a rating of fair, no grade should be recorded. Under "Remarks" mention anything that indicates interest, conduct, and proficiency of the pupil.
This card should be filed in folder for record of pupil (5-197). When the pupil is transferred to another school a copy of this card should be sent to the officer in charge of the school to which he is transferred.
