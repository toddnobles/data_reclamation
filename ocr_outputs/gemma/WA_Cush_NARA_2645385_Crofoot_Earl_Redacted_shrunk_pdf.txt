SPECIAL NOTE.
This form must be executed in duplicate when a child is transferred from a reservation to a nonreservation school. The Superintendent of the nonreservation school will retain the original for his files, and the duplicate shall be deposited in the reservation school records. The reservation superintendent should send to the Commissioner of Indian Affairs his certificate as provided by law. All the blanks must be properly filled in every case.
If the information called for on any part of the blank is not known, that fact should be stated. No space should be left unfilled. Whether the parents are living or dead, their names must be given.
The person who signs the blank as consenting to the transfer should indicate his relation to the applicant by marking out the word "parent," "guardian," or "next of kin," leaving unmarked only the title appropriate to the signer.

INDORSEMENTS.

The laws relating to the transfer of Indian children from reservations and schools are as follows:
That hereafter no Indian child shall be sent from any Indian reservation to a school beyond the State or Territory in which said reservation is situated without the voluntary consent of the father or mother of such child if either of them are living, and if neither of them are living without the voluntary consent of the next of kin of such child. Such consent shall be made before the agent of the reservation, and he shall send to the Commissioner of Indian Affairs his certificate that such consent has been voluntarily given before such child shall be removed from such reservation. And it shall be unlawful for any Indian agent or other employee of the Government to induce, or seek to induce, by withholding rations or by other improper means, the parents or next of kin of any Indian to consent to the removal of any Indian child beyond the limits of any reservation. (28 Stats., p. 906.)
Provided, That hereafter no Indian child shall be taken from any school in any State or Territory to a school in any other State against its will or without the written consent of its parents. (29 Stats., p. 348.)
That no Indian pupil under the age of fourteen years shall be transported at Government expense to any Indian school beyond the limits of the State or Territory in which the parents of such child reside or of the adjoining State or Territory. (35 Stat. L., 781.)

The rules provide that—
A pupil who has been regularly enrolled in a nonreservation school must not be taken to any other nonreservation school without the consent of both Superintendents and the Commissioner of Indian Affairs, and superintendents will be held to strict accountability for such pupils taken to their schools.
An Indian boy or girl 18 years old and over may, without the consent of parents or others, personally sign the application form on its being changed to suit the case; but in all cases where the parents are living they should first be consulted.

This form is to be used only in transfers from reservations or Indian schools to nonreservation schools.

Cushman Indian School
Tacoma, Wash., Jan. 16, 1920

Fred C. Morgan, Supt.
Colville Indian Agency
Nespelem, Washington

My dear Mr. Morgan:

I have to advise that Mrs. Crofoot has been here for the past three or four weeks trying to induce us to let her take home her son, Earl. I am now informed that Earl ran away yesterday and that his young brother said he went away with his mother. You will recall that she sent you a telegram in regard to this matter and that you wired me not to let her have him, as his father was his legal guardian. As far as I can ascertain in talking with Earl, he was perfectly satisfied here. Therefore I am convinced that it was a matter of spite on the part of Mrs. Crofoot in taking him away. I would be pleased if you could in some way get the boy returned to Cushman. Possibly his father can do this. We have the amount of $4.00 to Earl's credit which can be used toward defraying some of the expenses. We have no other money, however, that can be used for this purpose.

Mrs. Crofoot advised me that her lawyer said that according to the State Law, the boy being fourteen years of age, could choose his own guardian. I advised her that we had U. S. Government laws that took precedence over State Laws and that we would keep the boy here until his Reservation Superintendent and his father desired to withdraw him. I told her that you were a fair-minded man and that if she could convince you that she had a right to the boy, you might give your consent to his withdrawal.

She advised me that there was a public school within a mile and a half from her home and that Earl

(Page 2)
Morgan--2
had a pony and could attend this school.
However, I am in the opinion that if the boy gets home, he will never attend school again. It is my opinion that he should complete his year here and be transferred to Chemawa and complete the course there. I am convinced from talking with the boy that he prefers this. However, for the time being, he would enjoy a visit to his home and would soon tire of the visit and would want to return to Cushman.

I think this woman should be taught a lesson, at least by taking the boy away from her and returning him here, and if she comes here again, I will forbid her from seeing him, if it is possible.

Appreciating your help in this matter, I am

Yours very truly,

Special Agent in Charge.

My dear Mr. Morgan:

Supervisor West in Charge,
Colville Agency,
Nespelem, Wash.

Dear Sir:

I submit herewith for your endorsement, the applications of Mr. J. E. Crofoot of LaFleur, Wash., for the enrollment in this school of his five children, Earl, Edna, Lila, Lawrence, and Ernest aged 13, 12, 10, 8 and 7.

School having begun on the 9th instant, and we are already in our second week of regular school work, it is advisable that the children come soon that they may be able to make up back studies and catch up with the classes that they enter.

If you approve of their enrollment in the Cushman School, please endorsement the applications return them to me, and wire me "collect" where you wish tickets placed. The children will have to come alone owing to our transportation funds being almost exhausted.

Thanking you for an early reply, I am,

Yours very respectfully,
