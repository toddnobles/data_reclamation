Chandler, Arizona
May 13, 1920.

Mr. O. H. Keller
Tacoma, Washington

My dear Sir.

Your reply to my letter came & please accept my sincere thanks, I can't express the joy & happiness that I am to have my boy with us.
I hope that you have arrange his ticket for him, & perhaps on his way home now, the reason I wired the money is because its quicker & safer.
I thank you again for your help.
I am yours very respectfully,

Harvey A. Cawker.

DEPARTMENT OF THE INTERIOR
UNITED STATES INDIAN SERVICE

Skokomish Indian Reservation,
May 31, 1920.

Mr. Oscar H. Keller, Chief Clerk in Charge,
Cushman Indian School,
Tacoma, Wash.

Dear Mr. Keller:

Your letter of May 14th relative to the return of Allen Cawker to the Cushman School. Upon the receipt of your letter I called at the Peterson home but Mrs Peterson was away. Allen met me at the door and I told him that his father had sent money to your office for his return to Arizona. Also asked if he wished to go. He seemed very much pleased and promised me he would be off right a way.
I had supposed that he had returned to the school until Saturday evening when Mrs Peterson called upon me saying that Allen wanted to go but that Lucy Andrews had persuaded him not to go, but to come and live with them. Lucy Andrews- Allen is a cousin. Kimball Sherwood is her father and an uncle to the boy. Kimball would rather that the boy would go to his father, but will not take the iniative.

Yours very respectfully,

[Signature: William B. Miller]

Cushman Indian School,
Tacoma, Washington,
June 3, 1920.

Mr. Henry Allen,
Potlatch, Wash.

Dear Sir:

I understand that your wife has used her influence to keep Allen Cawker, who is a cousin, from going to live with his father.
You know that Allen's mother is not able to care for him and has not done so for years. His father is very anxious to have the boy with him as his sister Edith is there. Allen has an allotment in the south and it is legally the place where he should live.
We have money in the office sent by his father to pay his expenses to Arizona. Now that the father has provided a place for the boy, I think it best that he start for the south with as little delay as possible. If the boy does not have any money to pay his way to Cushman, Mr. Miller will advance it, I know, and he can be reimbursed later.

Very respectfully,

Clerk in Charge.

Chandler, Arizona

Dear Sir:

Before the receipt of your money and advice, your son, Allen Cawker, left school and went over to the Skokomish Reservation where his uncle resides.

Cushman Indian School
Tacoma, Wash., May 14, 1920

Mr. W. C. Miller
Skokomish Reservation
Potlatch, Washington

Dear Sir:

Allen Cawker left school yesterday with the Petersons and is probably now on the Skokomish Reservation. His father has sent funds, which reached me yesterday, for the payment of Allen's fare down to Arizona where his father lives.
The father desires to have the boy with him as it appears that his mother, Minnie Cawker, has practically given him no consideration for the last few years. I suggest that you look into the matter, and if there is no valid objection to his going to his father, will you arrange for the boy to be sent back to Cushman as early as possible so that we may send him to his father? I understand that he has wished to be with his father as his sister is down there with him.

Very respectfully,

Clerk in Charge.

Chandler, Arizona
May 3, 1920

Supt. C. Peel
Tacoma, Wash.

my dear sir.

This letter is regard to my boy Allen Cawker who is enrolled in your school, my desire is that you please allow him to come to me, I understand that his mother has neglected him for the past 3 years + at present no one knows her where about. I ask you thinking that its in your power to say what Allen should do since he is under your care, I believe that his mother would not object if she could be found, he has allotment of land here + it's really his home, he has no land there at his mother's home, it is my duty to care for him if his mother neglect him, last summer former Supt. Hammond agree to transfer him to Phoenix Indian School when school will be out this year, so I hope he may have told you about it, I hope to hear from you very soon in regard to this, when I hear from you + if he will come, I will wire to you some money to pay his fare + spending money on his way, let me know when he will leave there.
May I mention this that his sister Edith who had being there at your school + letter at Fort Sapwai Idaho, hospital came to me last June, Supt Dr. Breid of Fort Sapwai permitted her to come after he had found out that I was her father + that her mother could not be found, she is now enrolled at the Phoenix Indian School, that is why I do wish + hope that you permit my boy to be transfer there I will pay all his expense, + will wire the money as soon as I hear that he can come.
For 14 years I have not seen my boy, I am a Pima Indian living here on the reservation + I am in a position to take good care of my children, as I say Allen has allotment of 20 acres of farming land + 60 of desert land he will soon be old enough to farm his land I have being writing to him + he says he wants to come, please help me, + permit him to come to me, if you think it best for Allen to come to us, my daughter + I will be very happy if you will allow Allen to come.
I will close + please answer soon.
I remain yours very respectfully

Harvey A. Cawker
Chandler, Arizona.

Chandler, Arizona
Sept. 7, 1919.

Supt. C. H. Hammond
Tacoma, Wash.

Dear Sir.

This letter is regard to my son Allen Cawker whom I have learned that he is there in your school, my desire is to have my son come to me, if you will permit him to come, since I find out that he is there, I had written to him + he answered + paid he wanted to come to me, so it remains what you say, their mother can not be located for this reason Supt. Dr. Breid of Sapwai, Idaho has permitted my daughter to come here, it is for the same reason that I am asking you now about my boy since their mother can't be find, it seems right + proper that I as their father should have their care. I am willing to pay his rail-road fare from there to Phoenix Arizona, so if you grant my request, please let me know the cost of a ticket + I will forward the same.
I have being trying to find out where my children are since they left, but could never get in touch with them, + only recently when my daughter went to the sanatorium at Sapwai Idaho, Edith is now with me + is getting along nicely, + to be sure, both I + my daughter will be very happy if Allen can be permitted to join us here in Arizona.
I have not heard from Allen since July, I suppose he is out of school, if he is, please let me know where he is so I can write to him, hope to hear from you soon.

I remain yours truly
Harvey Cawker
Chandler, Arizona.

### Page 6: Indorsements

**INDORSEMENTS.**
Applicable laws relating to the transfer of Indian children from reservations and schools are as follows:
That hereafter no Indian child shall be sent from any Indian reservation to a school beyond the State or Territory in which said reservation is situated without the voluntary consent of the father or mother of such child if either of them are living... (28 Stats., p. 906.)
Provided, That hereafter no Indian child shall be taken from any school in any State or Territory to a school in any other State against its will or without the written consent of its parents. (29 Stats., p. 348.)
That no Indian pupil under the age of fourteen years shall be transported at Government expense to any Indian school beyond the limits of the State or Territory in which the parents of such child reside or of the adjoining State or Territory. (35 Stat. L., 781.)
The rules provide that—
A pupil who has been regularly enrolled in a nonreservation school must not be taken to any other nonreservation school without the consent of both superintendents and the Commissioner of Indian Affairs...
An Indian boy or girl 18 years old and over may, without the consent of parents or others, personally sign the application form...
