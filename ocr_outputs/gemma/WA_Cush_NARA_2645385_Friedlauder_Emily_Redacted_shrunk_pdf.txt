VACATION REQUEST FOR PUPIL.

We, the undersigned, respectfully request that leave of absence be granted Emily Friedlander from June 15, 1918 to Sept. 5, 1918 to enable her to visit her home. We hereby agree to pay all expenses, including round-trip railroad fares, sleeping-car fares, meals, lodging, stage fares, telegraph and telephone messages, and other incidental expenses connected with the visit. We further agree that said pupil shall be returned to the Cushman school on or before the last day of the leave of absence.

We further agree to deposit with the superintendent of the school sufficient money to defray all expenses to be incurred, and should the pupils, or any one of them, fail to return to school at such time as may be agreed upon by the parents or guardian and the superintendent of the school in which the pupils are enrolled, the amount of money remaining with the superintendent, necessary to the return transportation of the pupil or pupils so neglecting to return, shall be forfeited to the Government; provided, however, that in all cases where it can be shown that there were justifiable reasons for the failure of the return of the pupil the forfeiture of the funds shall not take place.

If the pupil neglecting to return is from a reservation, the superintendent of the reservation shall certify to the reasons for the nonreturn of the pupil or pupils. If a pupil's home is not on a reservation, then the certificate of some reputable, disinterested citizen of the community may be requested as to the reasons for the nonreturn of the pupil or pupils.

Dear Mr. Hammond:

I have your letter of February 13 advising me that Mrs. Francis, Emily Friedlander's mother, recently paid you a visit and requested permission to take Emily from school, claiming that she was sick and had been in the hospital for a long time. Mrs. Francis came to see me about visiting Cushman and I tried to advise her against going. Her visit was prompted by letters that were written home by various children who were attending your school from this reservation. Among them was a letter from Eddie Johnson who wrote a very discouraging letter to his father. I soon learned that it was no use to try to discourage her visit and advised her that she would find the girl all right and I hoped that she would learn more about your school and trusted that she would be better satisfied upon her return.

I assume that she has returned home by this time but I have not as yet seen her. I heartily approve of your action in not excusing Emily as there is no good reason why she should come home at this time. At the proper time I will advise Mrs. Francis that if she desires Emily to return for vacation that she may sign the official request and deposit the necessary funds. Mrs. Friedlander has been a source of annoyance since Emily left, continually bothering this office about Emily's condition. As stated above, I trust that her visit will bring good results and that the Indians in general will be better satisfied with Cushman and the splendid opportunities that their children have at your school.

Very respectfully,

[Signature]
Fred C. Morgan
Superintendent.

Supt. Fred C. Morgan,

Dear Mr. Morgan:

Emily Friedlander's mother complained that she found nits in her daughter's head. As I explained to Mrs. Francis, the matrons were obliged to keep the heads of the young girls clean, but the larger girls were supposed to be able to take care of their own heads - and were regularly inspected to see that they did do so.

Nevertheless, I visited the girls' home and the hospital and had a special inspection made with the results given in Mrs. Boyd's report of February 14th attached hereto.

I told Mrs. Francis to visit all departments and to enjoy herself while here. She appeared to do so and wandered about everywhere except in the sewing department which was temporarily closed owing to the illness of the seamstress.

I have also discussed with the boys the harm done by deluging their people with homesick letters and they seemed to be amazed, saying that they didn't intend to worry their parents. Eddie Moses denied flatly that he had written discouraging letters home.

Dear Mr. Morgan:

Emily Friedlander's mother, Mrs. Francis arrived today and requested permission to take Emily from school claiming that she was sick and had been in the hospital for some time.

As I wrote you, Emily has had a bad cold and tonsilitis, but has been kept in the hospital solely to enable the Doctor to cure the skin eruption on her hands. INDORSEMENTS.

The laws relating to the transfer of Indian children from reservations and schools are as follows:
That hereafter no Indian child shall be sent from any Indian reservation to a school beyond the State or Territory in which said reservation is situated without the voluntary consent of the father or mother of such child if either of them are living, and if neither of them are living without the voluntary consent of the next of kin of such child. Such consent shall be made before the agent of the reservation, and he shall send to the Commissioner of Indian Affairs his certificate that such consent has been voluntarily given before such child shall be removed from such reservation. And it shall be unlawful for any Indian agent or other employee of the Government to induce, or seek to induce, by withholding rations or by other improper means, the parents or next of kin of any Indian to consent to the removal of any Indian child beyond the limits of any reservation. (28 Stats., p. 906.)
Provided, That hereafter no Indian child shall be taken from any school in any State or Territory to a school in any other State against its will or without the written consent of its parents. (29 Stats., p. 348.)
That no Indian pupil under the age of fourteen years shall be transported at Government expense to any Indian school beyond the limits of the State or Territory in which the parents of such child reside or of the adjoining State or Territory. (35 Stat. L., 781.)

The rules provide that—
A pupil who has been regularly enrolled in a nonreservation school must not be taken to any other nonreservation school without the consent of both superintendents and the Commissioner of Indian Affairs, and superintendents will be held to strict accountability for such pupils taken to their schools.
An Indian boy or girl 18 years old and over may, without the consent of parents or others, personally sign the application form on its being changed to suit the case but in all cases where the parents are living they should first be consulted.

This form is to be used only in transfers from reservations or Indian schools to nonreservation schools.
