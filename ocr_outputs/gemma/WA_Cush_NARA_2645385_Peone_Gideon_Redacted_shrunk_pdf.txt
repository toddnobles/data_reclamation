[Stamp: CUSHMAN INDIAN SCHOOL RECEIVED NOV 2 8 1919 TACOMA, WASHINGTON]

November 25, 1919

E. H. Hammond, Superintendent,
Cushman Indian School, Tacoma.

My dear Mr. Hammond;-

I note your letter of November 21st, regarding Gideon Peone, and that you have given him permission to return home to attend public school as soon as funds for his transportation are received.

In accordance with your recommendation, I am forwarding check for $30.00 from this boy's funds, but in sending this money, I would advise that I doubt very much if Gideon will attend school, should he return to his home. I believe it is best for this boy, if his health permits, that he remain where he is now. Gideon's parents live in the Village of St. Ignatius, on this reservation, where there is a good public school, but when Gideon is at home, instead of attending school, he spends most of his time loafing, when he is not driving his father's automobile around the country, and I have no reason to believe that should he return at this time, he will not resume his former practices, but if you think it is best for the boy to return to the reservation, I leave the matter in your hands.

Very truly yours,
[Signature]: Theodore Sharp
Superintendent.

Cushman Indian School
Tacoma, Wash., Dec. 11, 1919

Mr. Theodore Sharp, Supt.
Flathead Indian Agency
Dixon, Montana

My dear Mr. Sharp:

This afternoon I purchased a ticket for Gideon to his home town, Ravalli.

Gideon is very much interested in engineering and I was in hopes that his interest would induce him to remain in school until the end of the year, but the necessary restraint of a boarding school was too irksome and he was dissatisfied and claimed that he wished to return home to attend the high school at his home town or at Polson. Finally I decided that this school would be much better off without his eternal grouch which not only interfered with his own work, but was a rallying point for discontent among his intimates.

Very truly yours,

5-240
VACATION REQUEST FOR PUPIL.

We, the undersigned, respectfully request that leave of absence be granted Gideon Peone from April 14 to Sept 5 1919 to enable him to visit his home. We hereby agree to pay all expenses, including round-trip railroad fares, sleeping-car fares, meals, lodging, stage fares, telegraph and telephone messages, and other incidental expenses connected with the visit. We further agree that said pupil shall be returned to the Cushman school on or before the last day of the leave of absence.

We further agree to deposit with the superintendent of the school sufficient money to defray all expenses to be incurred, and should the pupils, or any one of them, fail to return to school at such time as may be agreed upon by the parents or guardian and the superintendent of the school in which the pupils are enrolled, the amount of money remaining with the superintendent, necessary to the return transportation of the pupil or pupils so neglecting to return, shall be forfeited to the Government; provided, however, that in all cases where it can be shown that there were justifiable reasons for the failure of the return of the pupil the forfeiture of the funds shall not take place.

If the pupil neglecting to return is from a reservation, the superintendent of the reservation shall certify to the reasons for the nonreturn of the pupil or pupils. If a pupil's home is not on a reservation, then the certificate of some reputable, disinterested citizen of the community may be requested as to the reasons for the nonreturn of the pupil or pupils.

My dear Mr. Peon:

Dr. Kinnear has given me your letter of March 24th., to answer. As I wrote you, your son Gideon is convalescing from an attack of the flu, and that the doctor had placed him on special diet in hopes of bettering him. Cushman Indian School
Tacoma, Wash., Nov. 11, 1919

Mr. William Peone
St. Ignatius,
Montana

Dear Mr. Peone:

Your son Gideon is not in jail, nor has he been for a long while. Because he deserted, taking with him a young boy, he was court-martialed and sentenced to sleep in jail a week, I believe it was.

I do not permit the Disciplinarian to keep boys in jail depriving them of their schooling thereby.

Gideon has been well since he returned to school. Last week, he had a bad cold and I took him off the Engineering squad and allowed him to choose his job. He has not been sick.

I am sorry you think Gideon has been abused. He has not been. As I wrote you, Gideon has spent the money on deposit here for shoes and clothing and, consequently, can not return home until you send his fare.

Very truly yours,

Superintendent.
