Kettle Falls, Washington.
Route 1, Box 49
Aug. 23rd 1918.

Mr. E. H. Hammond,
Supt. Cushman Ind. school.

Dear Sir:
Your kind letter of August 13, the at hand and in reply wish to say. Samuel is taking treatment from the doctor for the after effects of the serious and well nigh fatal sicknesses of last term. We do not blame the faculty of Cushman for his illness but to the damp climate of the Sound country. And Mr. Hammond ask in all sincerity if you could not recommend and have the children transferred to some other good school in the interior or in a climate more suited to Samuels special temperament and state of health. According to his physician he needs special care and climate to avoid lung trouble.

Thanking you for your kindness in the past and asking your kind consideration, I beg to remain,

Sincerely
Mrs. Maggie Boyd

Cushman Indian School
Tacoma, Washington
June 28, 1918.

Mrs. Maggie Boyd
Route #1 Box 49
Kettle Falls, Washington.

Dear Mrs. Boyd:
Your letter of the 25th., is at hand. As I wrote you day before yesterday, Sam is getting along all right and is very anxious to return home for the vacation. The other children are well at this date and appear to be enjoying themselves. We try to keep them busy enough to keep them out of mischief but not to overtax them. They have plenty of time for play as well.

Don't worry about your children, Mrs. Boyd. If anything serious happens to them I will advise you promptly. Sam has been ill repeatedly but at only one time did our physician report his condition as critical.

Your other children have had various spells of grippe, measles, mumps and so forth but are to all appearances enjoying themselves. They will, of course, feel downhearted at Sam's leaving them - but we will try to make them happy.

Very sincerely yours
Superintendent

Cushman Indian School
Tacoma, Washington
June 26, 1918.

Mrs. Maggie Boyd
Kettle Falls, Washington.

Dear Mrs. Boyd:
Samuel is again convalescing and I hope will soon be fully recovered. He was discharged from the hospital some time ago but after a few days he returned with an attack of pleurisy which later turned into pleura-pneumonia.

We have him on the road to recovery however. A few weeks or a couple of months at home would help him recover more quickly.

Do not worry about Samuel. We gave him the best of care and nursing and he is up and about and can travel any time you send for him.

The other children are well.

Very truly yours,
Superintendent

Cushman Indian School,
Tacoma, Washington,
Feb. 15, 1918.

Mrs. Maggie Boyd,
Route 1, Box 49,
Kettle Falls, Wash.

Dear Mrs. Boyd:
The children have not been sick this winter except with bad colds once or twice until just now. Juanita has German Measles, but as they last only nine days, she will be out February 20th.

Sam went to the hospital with an attack of rheumatism on the 12th, but I expect he will be out in a short time. The doctor and the trained nurse will give him every possible attention, and I anticipate that by the time you receive this letter, he will be out again.

You can trust me to look after your children's health, and if they become really sick, I will notify you immediately.

Yours very sincerely,
Superintendent

April 18, 1917.

Mrs. Fannie Smith Boyd,
Kettle Falls, Wash.

Dear Madam:
In answer to your letter of April 14, I have to say that your application for the enrollment of your five children was accepted and we can take them any time after the 1st of July, but we would prefer to have them from the 1st to the 20th of September.

If you will notify me just when you want them to come, it is very probable that I can place transportation for them. I cannot say positive that I can do that until later in the season, but whenever you make your application, I will then be in a position to give you definite information as to paying their transportation.

Very respectfully
Superintendent.

Cushman Indian School
Tacoma, Washington,
June 1, 1918.

Dear Mr. Hammond,-
At Dr. Kinnear's request I am sending you a report of Millard Lang's case and as Samuel Boyd's is similar tho not so serious, I have included his.

Dr. Kinnear tells me that a large percentage of pneumonia cases this year are being followed by the forming of pus as in these cases.

Out at the Base hospital they have many cases all of which are at once drained and which is doubtless the best procedure, but Dr. Kinnear has the parents to consider and he has reason to think that both of these cases are yielding to the treatment and the operation may not be necessary. He, however, thinks that you had better notify Millard's parents so that if drainage becomes necessary their consent may be gained.

Personally, I believe that Millard is improving since yesterday morning. I think that there is no doubt but that Millard's last attack was brought on by the needless exposure. Until he became serious this time, he was a rather unruly patient but he now seems to realize that he brought his trouble on himself.
