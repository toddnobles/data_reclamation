Antoine Pariseau

DEPARTMENT OF THE INTERIOR
UNITED STATES INDIAN SERVICE
Flathead Agency - Dixon, Mont.

April 17, 1920.

C. V. Peel, Special Agent in Charge,
Cushman Indian School,
Tacoma, Wash.

Dear Mr. Peel;-

Your letter of April 12, 1920, concerning the desire of Gus White that the Parizeau boys be sent home to assist him in work, is received.

It is not believed essential nor desirable that the Parizeau boys should be returned to assist Gus White, who is their stepfather. Gus White, it is well known in the community in which he lives, does very little work, and he certainly does not need the assistance of these young boys to help him do the little labor which he now performs.

Your letter, as above referred to, is the first intimation this office has had that Patrick Parizeau has been placed in a reform school at Monroe. For our files, will you kindly advise me the circumstances under which this boy has been placed in the reform school, and the length of term to which he has been sentenced?

Very truly yours,

Theodore Sharp
Superintendent.

TS/R

Cushman Indian School
Tacoma, Wash., April 22, 1920

Theodore Sharp, Supt.
Flathead Indian Agency
Dixon, Montana

Dear Mr. Sharp:

I have your inquiry of April 17, 1920, concerning Patrick Parizeau and in reply thereto will state that he appears to have a decided weakness for stealing; that he has been punished at different times for his thefts at the school. In the early part of the year, he was apprehended by the Tacoma police for stealing some property from a cabin, some distance from the school. He admitted the theft, but was not prosecuted. The juvenile officer took no action, but advised Pat that if he was found guilty of any more thefts he would eventually land in the reform school.

But Pat could not resist stealing. He, with three other boys, broke into a store near the school and appropriated all the goods they could carry away. The police took them in charge and hailed them into the juvenile Court, there Pat and another boy were sentenced to the Reform School at Monroe, Washington, for a term of one to fifteen years. One of the other two boys was sentenced to the Reform School at Chehalis, but the sentence was suspended upon the application of one of our day school teachers who voluntarily assumed the responsibility for this boy's future conduct.

The fourth boy escaped punishment by the Court.

Very respectfully,

Special Agent in Charge.

Cushman Indian School
Tacoma, Wash., Sept. 11, 1919

Mrs. Angelic White
Box 370
Ronan, Montana

Dear Mrs. White:

I received your letter and Pat's regarding his return to school.

I have sufficient of Pat's money on deposit here to send an employee for him in case he doen't return promptly and I shall use Pat's money for this purpose unless he does return. Louis and Antoine are at Cushman and are well.

It is very dangerous for a boy of Pat's age to be allowed to do as he pleases and it would be for his advantage to learn that there is a higher law than his own wishes. Please advise Pat that unless he returns within a week, I shall use his money to return him to school.

Very sincerely yours,

Superintendent.
