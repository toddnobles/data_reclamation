Cushman Indian School
Tacoma, Wash., Jan. 27, 1920

Dr. Eugene W. Hill, Supt.
Taholah,
Washington

Dear Sir:

About December 8, 1919, there was mailed to Dr. Benson, Superintendent, Taholah, check No. 1027 payable to his order for $15.00. This check represented a transfer of funds for Jennie George, an Indian pupil, who had left Cushman permanently.

On December 31st, this check had not been presented for payment, besides no official receipt has been furnished me showing that this amount had been taken up on the Superintendent's accounts at Taholah.

Kindly advise me if the check has come into your possession, or any information pertaining thereto.

Very respectfully,

Special Agent in Charge.

Dear Sir,

In reply to yours as to check of Jennie George,

I would advise as follows,

This matter was not conducted as I would have considered regular, that is through the accounts, Jennies check deposited and check drawn here for the amount.

On my arrival here this check for Jennie George was indorsed by Dr. Benson direct to the Supt, thus, "Pay to order of Supt, E.W.Hill", but did not sign his name, also it was someway in the hands of the girl for me to sign after deposit at one of the stores, and returned to this Office as unendorsed.

Dr Benson had left the Station and I forwarded check for his endorsement to Seattle, but was returned here again, The check finally reached Dr. Benson on my reforwarding same to him and signature secured.

It had been cashed at a local store here and so with these signatures it was passed on and has been sent to the merchants bank cashing the check, the money having been paid Jennie previously. I have investigated the matter and find that it has been deposited long enough that it should return to you

Of course no book transaction appears in this matter as it has been conducted.

Very truly,

Superintendent.

TAHOLAH INDIAN AGENCY,
Taholah, Washington, Dec. 3, 1919.

Supt. E. S. Hammond,
Cushman Trades School,

My dear Mr. Hammond:

The parents of Jennie George came to my office this morning and requested that they be permitted to bring Jennie home to attend the day school here. They have removed recently from Chenois Creek to Taholah, so they are near the school now.

I told them that I was opposed to taking children out of school and putting them into others, as these rapid-fire changes are detrimental. However, the school rules provide that when children can be supported at home, they shall not be kept at boarding schools.

I will leave this matter for your decision. The circumstances differ from those in the Cole case, which requires the protection of your school to guard the morals of Catherine.

I probably owe you an apology for the heat manifest in my last letter concerning Julia Cole's insistence that Catherine be permitted to return because her foster mother's heart was breaking. Julia is the worst ever, and this is saying a good deal for her considering that she is a Quinaielt.

I am hoping to be relieved in a few days now, so this is likely my last communication to you. You have my best wishes for a safe course through the troublous times that enshroud a superintendent always.

Respectfully,
[signature]
Otis O. Benson
Supt. & Physician.

EXTRACTS FROM SCHOOL RULES.
If the facilities of a school are not needed as provided in the above paragraph, children showing any Indian blood, whose parents live on an Indian reservation, and who if debarred from Government schools could not obtain an education, may be enrolled in the day and boarding schools of their reservation; children, however, may not be transferred to nonreservation schools without special permission of the Commissioner of Indian Affairs.
Before enrolling a pupil superintendents must satisfy themselves whether he has left any other school without permission and whether he has been expelled for cause from any other school. INDORSEMENTS.

Applicable laws relating to the transfer of Indian children from reservations and schools are as follows:
That hereafter no Indian child shall be sent from any Indian reservation to a school beyond the State or Territory in which said reservation is situated without the voluntary consent of the father or mother of such child if either of them are living, and if neither of them are living without the voluntary consent of the next of kin of such child. Such consent shall be made before the agent of the reservation, and he shall send to the Commissioner of Indian Affairs his certificate that such consent has been voluntarily given before such child shall be removed from such reservation. And it shall be unlawful for any Indian agent or other employee of the Government to induce, or seek to induce, by withholding rations or by other improper means, the parents or next of kin of any Indian to consent to the removal of any Indian child beyond the limits of any reservation. (28 Stats., p. 906.)

Provided, That hereafter no Indian child shall be taken from any school in any State or Territory to a school in any other State against its will or without the written consent of its parents. (29 Stats., p. 348.)

That no Indian pupil under the age of fourteen years shall be transported at Government expense to any Indian school beyond the limits of the State or Territory in which the parents of such child reside or of the adjoining State or Territory. (35 Stat. L., 781.)

The rules provide that—
A pupil who has been regularly enrolled in a nonreservation school must not be taken to any other nonreservation school without the consent of both superintendents and the Commissioner of Indian Affairs, and superintendents will be held to strict accountability for such pupils taken to their schools.
An Indian boy or girl 18 years old and over may, without the consent of parents or others, personally sign the application form on its being changed to suit the case; but in all cases where the parents are living they should first be consulted.

Compliance must be given to all orders and instructions promulgated in connection with the new course of study issued December 1, 1915.

The information as to public school facilities called for in the certificate of the Superintendent (third page hereof) must be given, and all superintendents are especially directed to exhaust every means to secure enrollment of Indian children in the State public schools, pursuant to instructions heretofore issued in this regard.

RECORD CARD—PREVOCATIONAL DIVISION.

Each girl should devote 15 weeks to the work of this department during the three prevocational years. When she has accomplished the work as outlined in the course of study in a satisfactory manner, a suitable rating should be recorded. Ratings must be based on a practical application as well as a knowledge of the principles involved.
