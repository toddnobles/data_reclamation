(Typed letter)
Cushman Indian School
Tacoma, Wash., April 9, 1920

Mr. T. F. Johnson
Kasaan,
Alaska

Dear Sir:

I have to advise you that your son, Daniel, recently had the measles, and it has left him with weak eyes. As a consequence he has been unable to attend school for the past two months. He is becoming somewhat restless and has expressed a desire several times to go home. I told him to write you in regard to the matter and get your advice.

Daniel was in my office this morning and expressed his desire to go in the city of Tacoma and get a position. I advised him that I would see the physician and see whether he would be able to return to the school room and also write you and explain matters. I told Daniel that I did not feel as though I could give permission for him to leave the premises without some word from you.

Please advise me if you desire him to come home during the summer or continue at the school. This school will be permanently closed in June and it will be necessary to transfer the pupils to either the Tulalip School, near Everett, Washington, or to the Chemawa School, near Salem, Oregon. I would suggest Tulalip as it is nearer his home. Tulalip is a splendid school and he can get just as good schooling there as here.

Please advise me at your earliest convenience your desire in this matter and if you want Daniel to come home, send the necessary funds for his transportation.

Very truly yours,

Special Agent in Charge.

Cushman Indian School
Tacoma, Wash., Nov. 21, 1919

Mr. T. F. Johnson
Kasaan,
Alaska

Dear Mr. Johnson:

Daniel Franklin Johnson was in school at the time the receipt was mailed you October 21st.

During the summer months Daniel was given permission with other large boys to work in the Puyallup Valley to earn spending money. He returned at the re-opening of school and remained in school for a week or so when he again tried to reach Alaska, but was headed off at Seattle and forced to return after a week's absence. This was his third and last attempt.

I had a long talk with him and told him that since he would not listen to me he would have to be court-martialed by the student-body. The Battalion officers did court-martial him and sentenced him to go to school and to the Industrial Departments under guard and to sleep in jail one month. I approved this sentence but after a week I pardoned Daniel and his comrade as they both had decided that it was better to do what was right. Since then Daniel has been working hard. I gave him a five-weeks course in shoe and harness repairing, which he enjoyed and in which he did some very excellent work. He has been detailed to the Engineering Department, in which he is particularly interested. His work in this department is creditable. His instructor is taking an interest in him and if Daniel will give heed to our advice and our instruction, he will not regret the time that he spent at Cushman.

He is well and much stouter than when he came to school. I will have him write you himself if he has not done so.

Very sincerely yours,

Superintendent.

INDORSEMENTS.

Applicable laws relating to the transfer of Indian children from reservations and schools are as follows:
That hereafter no Indian child shall be sent from any Indian reservation to a school beyond the State or Territory in which said reservation is situated without the voluntary consent of the father or mother of such child if either of them are living, and if neither of them are living without the voluntary consent of the next of kin of such child. Such consent shall be made before the agent of the reservation, and he shall send to the Commissioner of Indian Affairs his certificate that such consent has been voluntarily given before such child shall be removed from such reservation. And it shall be unlawful for any Indian agent or other employee of the Government to induce, or seek to induce, by withholding rations or by other improper means, the parents or next of kin of any Indian child to consent to the removal of any Indian child beyond the limits of any reservation. (28 Stats., p. 906.)

Provided, That hereafter no Indian child shall be taken from any school in any State or Territory to a school in any other State against its will or without the written consent of its parents. (29 Stats., p. 348.)

That no Indian pupil under the age of fourteen years shall be transported at Government expense to any Indian school beyond the limits of the State or Territory in which the parents of such child reside or of the adjoining State or Territory. (35 Stat. L., 781.)

The rules provide that--
A pupil who has been regularly enrolled in a nonreservation school must not be taken to any other nonreservation school without the consent of both superintendents and the Commissioner of Indian Affairs, and superintendents will be held to strict accountability for such pupils taken to their schools.

An Indian boy or girl 18 years old and over may, without the consent of parents or others, personally sign the application form on its being changed to suit the case; but in all cases where the parents are living they should first be consulted.

Compliance must be given to all orders and instructions promulgated in connection with the new course of study issued December 1, 1915.

The information as to public school facilities called for in the certificate of the Superintendent (third page hereof) must be given, and all superintendents are especially directed to exhaust every means to secure enrollment of Indian children in the State public schools, pursuant to instructions heretofore issued in this regard.

My dear Mr. Johnson:

You have, by this time, received the Official Receipt for the payment of the tuition for the second quarter.

Daniel is becoming very fat, but occasionally insists that he has consumption. After the close of school, he and the McAlpin boys left without permission and picked berries in the country for several days before I found them. Daniel was punished reasonably for this misbehavior and wrote you a most heart-rending letter declaring that he was dying of consumption and begging you to send him home. He is, as I have said, becoming very stout and I hope in time he will change his attitude as you know he will benefit very much from the reasonable discipline and the excellent work of industrial instruction given him.

If you know of any more boys, fourteen years old, or older, who should be in school, I shall be glad to promise them every opportunity to make good here.

Cushman Indian School
Tacoma, Washington
April 8, 1919.

Mr. T. F. Johnson
Kasaan, Alaska.

Dear Sir:

Your son, David Franklin, arrived safely at Cushman. Daniel will naturally feel homesick and will probably consider a return to Alaska any time within the next six months. I shall, however, try to interest him in his work and studies so that he will become attached to the school and will strive to profit by the advantages offered him. If he leaves school without my knowledge or consent, I shall have him traced and brought back until his three year term has expired.

The Government charges parents who are able to support their children a tuition fee of $200.00 a year. We have a number of Alaskans whose parents pay their tuition at Cushman. If you are in a position to do so, I advise you to do it as it will undoubtedly exert a good influence over Daniel and encourage him to make the best of his opportunities.

Daniel arrived at the school with a bad cold and told me that he did not think he would stay long as he had consumption. I had our doctor examine him immediately and found that his lungs weren't affected in the least degree, so you need not worry if he writes you that he has consumption. It will mean that he is homesick and wishes you to take him out of school.

Very truly yours,

Superintendent

Supt Cushman School
Tacoma wash

Dear Sir your favor of april 8 20 at hand, and contents noted!

Enclosed yours will Please find check for the amount of $50.00, I will send check for $50.00 Every three months In future,

I note what you say regarding my Boy. I dont think he will agree to take him out of school, as he would know that I would not do any thing of the kind, — But he may take the liberty to leave on his own accord some time, so you should not allow him too much liberty, I hope that he will take an Interest in his Studies and his work as well, so that he may amount to something when he grows up,

Yours Truly
T. F. Johnson

VACATION REQUEST FOR PUPIL.

We, the undersigned, respectfully request that leave of absence be granted Daniel Franklin Johnson from June 19th - 1919 to Sept. 5th - 1919 to enable him to visit his home. We hereby agree to pay all expenses, including round-trip railroad fares, sleeping-car fares, meals, lodging, stage fares, telegraph and telephone messages, and other incidental expenses connected with the visit. We further agree that said pupil shall be returned to the Cushman school on or before the last day of the leave of absence.

We further agree to deposit with the superintendent of the school sufficient money to defray all expenses to be incurred, and should the pupils, or any one of them, fail to return to school at such time as may be agreed upon by the parents or guardian and the superintendent of the school in which the pupils are enrolled, the amount of money remaining with the superintendent, necessary to the return transportation of the pupil or pupils so neglecting to return, shall be forfeited to the Government; provided, however, that in all cases where it can be shown that there were justifiable reasons for the failure of the return of the pupil the forfeiture of the funds shall not take place.

If the pupil neglecting to return is from a reservation, the superintendent of the reservation shall certify to the reasons for the nonreturn of the pupil or pupils. If a pupil's home is not on a reservation, then the certificate of some reputable, disinterested citizen of the community may be requested as to the reasons for the nonreturn of the pupil or pupils.

Date: 5-15-19

NOTE TO DISBURSING OFFICERS.—All receipts under this agreement must be accounted for officially as Individual Indian Money. However, any forfeitures must be transferred to Miscellaneous Receipts, Class I, and deposited in the Treasury to the credit of the United States.

SEATTLE, WASH.. April 23, 1919.

LOANS NEGOTIATED
INVESTMENTS MADE

[Handwritten notes: Pupil / File / Daniel Johnson]

Supt. Cushman Indian School,
Tacoma, Wash.

Dear Sir:-

I received letter under date of the 31st ult. from your assistant Clerk, inclosing application for the enrollment of Daniel Johnson, an Indian boy from Kasaan, Alaska. I could not furnish all the information, so I sent the application to T. F. Johnson, father of the boy, at Kasaan, and it has been returned to me today. I therefore inclose it herewith, with the information furnished by the father. The boy's father and mother are both living. I sincerely hope that the boy will get along well.

If I can be of any service to you or the boy, kindly let me know. The father is extremely anxious for the boy to be diligent and to learn. I was especially impressed with the boy's inquisitive nature. I found that he was very observing.

Yours truly,
C. L. Parker
