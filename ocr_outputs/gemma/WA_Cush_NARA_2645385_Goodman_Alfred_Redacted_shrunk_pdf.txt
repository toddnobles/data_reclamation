[Handwritten name at top: Alf. Goodman]
**Cushman Indian School**
**Tacoma, Wash., Sept. 10, 1919**

**Mrs. Josephine Goodman**
**811 South 11th Street**
**Tacoma, Washington.**

**Dear Mrs. Goodman:**

I am in receipt of information from your former Superintendent, Dr. O. O. Benson, regarding your status.

Specific instructions from the Commissioner of Indian Affairs are that:
"The children of Indians who have received their patents in fee to their allotments are to be excluded from enrollment in a Government school supported from gratuity appropriations, unless in a given case the Indian child is a Federal ward, irrespective of the status of the parents."

When your children were accepted, you were a ward of the Government. On receiving a patent in fee, you became a full-fledged citizen; and as your children are not wards of the Government, they are not entitled to enrollment in a Government Indian School.

You are, therefore, advised that they will not be retained at Cushman without special permission from the Commissioner of Indian Affairs.

A copy of our Annual Calender containing the new regulations for the admission of children to Government Indian schools is attached hereto. Your attention is particularly invited to Rules 1, 2, and 3.

**Very truly yours,**
**Superintendent.**

KHH:MG
Enc.
