Cushman Indian School
Tacoma, Wash.,
May 27, 1920/

Mr. Ben Benson,
Chignik, Alaska.

Dear Sir:

Replying to your letter of May 2, 1920, in regard to your daughter, I have to inform you that this school closed permanently on May 14th. Elsie was sent to Mrs. Lunden at Astoria, Oregon.

If you wish to place her in another Indian school, you should make application for her enrollment in Chemawa Indian School, Chemawa, Oregon. Mr. Harwood Hall is the Superintendent of Chemawa school.

Yours respectfully,

OHK-GES
Clerk in Charge.

[Stamp: CUSHMAN INDIAN SCHOOL RECEIVED MAY 26 1920 TACOMA, WASHINGTON]

Chignik, Alaska, May 2, 1920

Superintendent Cushman School,
Tacoma, Wash.,

Dear sir:

On her way to Chignik, Alaska, Mrs. Osmund called at the school to see my daughter and was informed that the school would close in June on account of not sufficient funds. If this is correct, I would like to have you send my daughter to Mrs. Lundine, 385 Twenty seventh street, Astoria, Oregon. In any event, I would like to have her sent there for her vacation.

When I sent her to the Cushman School I signed for three years, which time will expire next October. If possible, I would like my daughter to remain for three years longer under the same conditions as before. I can not very well afford to pay for this as I am now paying for two other children here in Alaska.

In the event of the school remaining open, I would appreciate it very much if you would notify me. Thanking you in advance, I am,

Very truly yours,

Ben Benson

VACATION REQUEST FOR PUPIL.

[Stamp: CUSHMAN INDIAN SCHOOL RECEIVED JUN 3 - 1918 TACOMA, WASHINGTON]
no deposit (handwritten)

We, the undersigned, respectfully request that leave of absence be granted Elsie Brown
from June 1st to October 1st to enable her to visit her home. We hereby agree to pay all expenses, including round-trip railroad fares, sleeping-car fares, meals, lodging, stage fares, telegraph and telephone messages, and other incidental expenses connected with the visit. We further agree that said pupil shall be returned to the Cushman Trade school on or before the last day of the leave of absence.

We further agree to deposit with the superintendent of the school sufficient money to defray all expenses to be incurred, and should the pupils, or any one of them, fail to return to school at such time as may be agreed upon by the parents or guardian and the superintendent of the school in which the pupils are enrolled, the amount of money remaining with the superintendent, necessary to the return transportation of the pupil or pupils so neglecting to return, shall be forfeited to the Government; provided, however, that in all cases where it can be shown that there were justifiable reasons for the failure of the return of the pupil the forfeiture of the funds shall not take place.

If the pupil neglecting to return is from a reservation, the superintendent of the reservation shall certify to the reasons for the nonreturn of the pupil or pupils. If a pupil's home is not on a reservation, then the certificate of some reputable, disinterested citizen of the community may be requested as to the reasons for the nonreturn of the pupil or pupils.

Father.
Mother.
Guardian: Mr & Mrs Henry Moy

Date: May 24th 1918

NOTE TO DISBURSING OFFICERS.—All receipts under this agreement must be accounted for officially as Individual Indian Money. However, any forfeitures must be transferred to Miscellaneous Receipts, Class I, and deposited in the Treasury to the credit of the United States.

Cushman Trade School
Tacoma, Wash.

Dear Sir:-

Your letter of May 10th regarding my sister-in-law Elsie Brown has been received. As she is to stay with us this summer, we are taking the liberty of signing for her vacation. Her father has left her with her Aunt quite while and the usual method of the older folks are to let the child work all summer and take most of the earnings, we therefore hope by having her stay with us, when she

Dear Mrs. Anderson:-

As you will see by the enclosed letter and envelope, I answered promptly your enquiry about Elsie Benson and returned your ten dollars to you. A Mr. or Mrs. Anderson opened the letter and returned it and its contents to me.

Elsie's father has written her since that she could visit you and she may do so on condition of being promptly returned to school, September 5th., 1918.

I am sorry she has missed so much of her visit but as I am responsible for the children entrusted to me, I must insist on the parent's written consent to a pupil's visiting friends, or relatives.

Very sincerely yours,

EHH.
Superintendent.

Cushman School
Dear Sir:-

Some time ago I wrote to you for information about Elsie Benson, but have received no answer.

Now as the term is soon coming to an end I would be very glad to know if she is going to get her vacations, for I think she should as she was disappointed last year. I have noticed from her letters that she would be very glad to come here, and you surely could trust her to our care. She would be sent back for the time that school opens again.

I am here enclosing ten dollars for Elsie's fare, if not enough please let me know, in time so that I may send more.
